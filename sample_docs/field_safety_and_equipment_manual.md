## Scope

This manual covers personal protective equipment, working at height, confined
space entry, traffic management, electrical safety and test equipment care for
field engineers on the access network.

## Personal protective equipment

The minimum kit for any street work is a high visibility jacket to class 2,
safety footwear with a midsole, and safety glasses. Gloves are selected for the
task rather than worn by default, because a glove that is wrong for the task is
worse than no glove.

Insulated gloves rated to 1000 V are required inside any powered cabinet. Check
the gloves for pinholes by rolling them closed and squeezing before every use.
Insulated gloves are withdrawn from service after 6 months regardless of
apparent condition.

## Pole testing

Test every pole before climbing, every time, including a pole you climbed the
previous day. Strike the pole at knee height and at shoulder height with a
pole test hammer and listen for a dull response.

A pole that fails the test is marked with a red band and reported the same day.
Do not climb a poled marked with a red band under any circumstances, including
to recover equipment.

Poles are formally inspected every 10 years. The inspection date is stamped on
the pole plate near the base.

## Ladder work

Set a ladder at a 75 degree angle, which is one unit out for every four units
up. The ladder must extend at least 1 metre above the stepping off point.

A ladder must be footed by a second person or secured at the top. Working from
an unfooted, unsecured ladder is the most common cause of serious injury in
access network work.

Do not work from a ladder in wind above 25 miles per hour, or where the wind is
gusting enough to move the ladder when unloaded.

## Confined space entry

A footway chamber deeper than 1.5 metres is a confined space. Confined space
entry requires a gas test before entry, a second person at the surface at all
times, and a tripod and harness where the chamber is deeper than 2 metres.

Test for oxygen, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulphide and flammable gas. Oxygen
below 19.5 per cent or above 23.5 per cent means do not enter. Re-test every
20 minutes while the chamber is occupied.

Gas detectors are bump tested before every shift and fully calibrated every
6 months.

## Traffic management

Any work within 1 metre of a live carriageway requires signing, lighting and
guarding. On a road with a speed limit above 40 miles per hour a lane closure
is required rather than a coned working space.

Cones are set at 1 metre spacing on the taper and 9 metre spacing along the
working space. The taper length is 15 metres on a 30 mile per hour road and
30 metres on a 40 mile per hour road.

## Street cabinet electrical safety

Two people are required when working inside a powered street cabinet. Check for
water ingress before touching any equipment and do not open the cabinet in
heavy rain.

The cabinet mains supply is 230 V single phase, protected by a 16 amp fuse in
the supply pillar. Isolate at the pillar, not at the cabinet, before any work on
the power shelf.

## Street cabinet battery backup

The cabinet runs on mains power with a battery backup. Batteries give about
6 hours of backup at normal load on a cabinet fitted with the revision 3 battery
shelf, which has been standard since 2021.

A healthy battery string reads between 52 and 54 volts on float charge. Replace
the battery string if any single battery reads below 11.5 volts after a full
charge.

Batteries contain sulphuric acid. Wear safety glasses and acid resistant gloves
when handling a battery, and never lift a battery alone.

## Working near overhead power

Maintain a minimum clearance of 3 metres from any overhead power line at all
times, including the ladder, the engineer and any cable being handled.

Where a telecom pole carries a power line attachment, the work requires a permit
from the power network operator. A permit takes 10 working days, so plan the
visit rather than attending and turning back.

## Manual handling

Two people are required for any load above 25 kilograms, including a cable drum,
a battery, and a joint closure filled with gel.

A footway chamber lid is lifted with a lid lifter, never by hand and never with
a screwdriver. Chamber lids are a leading cause of back and hand injury.

## Test equipment calibration

Calibration intervals are as follows:

- Optical power meter: 12 months
- OTDR: 12 months
- Copper line tester: 12 months
- Gas detector: 6 months
- Insulated glove inspection: 6 months
- Torque wrench: 24 months

Check the calibration label at the start of every shift. Equipment more than
30 days past its calibration date must not be used for any acceptance test.

## Test equipment care

Store an OTDR in its case with the connector capped. The single most common
cause of an OTDR going out of service is a damaged front panel connector from
transport without a cap.

Clean the OTDR launch connector before every trace. A dirty launch connector
produces a large first event that masks a genuine fault at the near end.

## Lone working

Lone working is permitted for work at ground level with no confined space entry,
no work at height, and no work inside a powered cabinet.

Book on and book off with the control centre at the start and end of every lone
working visit. A missed book off triggers a welfare check after 60 minutes.

## Incident reporting

Report any injury, near miss or damage to third party property on the same day,
before the end of the shift. An incident reported the following day is treated
as a late report and is investigated separately.

Photograph damage to third party property before starting any remedial work.

## Adverse weather

Stop work at height in wind above 25 miles per hour. Stop all external work in
an electrical storm and move away from poles and chambers.

Do not open a footway chamber during heavy rain where surface water will enter
the chamber. Water in a chamber turns a routine joint repair into a confined
space and water ingress problem.

## Vehicle and van stock

Secure all loads in the van, including small tools. An unsecured tool becomes a
projectile in a collision.

Check van stock levels at the end of every shift. The most commonly missing
items are one-click cleaners, dust caps and 5 dB attenuators, which are also the
items whose absence most often causes a revisit.
